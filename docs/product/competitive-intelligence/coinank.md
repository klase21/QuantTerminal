## 3. Information Architecture

Navigation is metric and market-category oriented. Hierarchy is dashboard/table first. Grouping is by derivatives dataset and exchange/symbol. Progressive disclosure is moderate. Density is high. Search/filtering are necessary. Workspace is monitoring-oriented, not research-oriented.

## 4. Visualization

Charts, tables, and heatmaps dominate. Cards summarize metrics. Timelines are available through charts. Network graphs are not core. Animations are minimal. Typography is dense. Spacing is compact. Color language is market-direction and heatmap oriented.

## 5. Decision Support

Users reach conclusions by scanning derivatives conditions and comparing metrics. Evidence is metric-based. Reasoning is user-driven. Historical context exists through charts. Confidence and warnings depend on source/methodology presentation. Counter-evidence requires multi-metric comparison.

## 6. UX

Learning curve is medium. Onboarding assumes crypto derivatives literacy. Interaction is chart, table, filter, search, and category navigation. Customization is limited. Desktop is primary. Mobile is monitoring-oriented. Accessibility can be challenged by density.

## 7. Strengths

- Compact derivatives metric access.
- Broad crypto market scanning.
- Useful exchange/symbol comparisons.
- High data density.

## 8. Weaknesses

- Can be visually crowded.
- Source transparency may not be prominent.
- Less explanatory product layer.
- Limited progressive disclosure.
