## 3. Information Architecture

Navigation groups by derivatives concepts: liquidations, open interest, funding, long/short, heatmaps, and exchange views. Hierarchy is metric-first. Progressive disclosure is moderate: top tables and charts lead to asset/exchange detail. Density is high. Search and filtering are symbol/exchange oriented. Workspace is dashboard browsing rather than persistent research.

## 4. Visualization

Charts and tables dominate. Heatmaps are a major product pattern. Cards summarize headline metrics. Timelines appear through liquidation and funding history. Network graphs are not central. Animations are minimal. Typography and spacing prioritize density. Color language is strong but can overload users if not carefully interpreted.

## 5. Decision Support

Users reach conclusions by comparing leverage metrics, liquidation levels, OI, and funding. Evidence is metric-based. Reasoning is mostly user-provided. Historical context exists in charts. Confidence and warnings are not always explicit. Counter-evidence depends on comparing multiple metrics.

## 6. UX

Learning curve is medium. Onboarding assumes crypto derivatives familiarity. Interaction is dashboard, filter, chart, table, and heatmap driven. Customization is moderate. Productivity is high for leverage monitoring. Desktop is primary; mobile works better for monitoring than deep analysis. Accessibility can be challenged by dense color-heavy views.

## 7. Strengths

- Derivatives-specific information density.
- Liquidation heatmap pattern.
- Quick OI/funding scanning.
- Exchange comparison.
- Metric grouping around trader questions.

## 8. Weaknesses

- Can feel visually crowded.
- Metric definitions and source quality need strong explanation.
- Dense color usage can imply certainty.
- Less suited for deeper evidence narratives.
