## 3. Information Architecture

Navigation is metric, dashboard, asset, and research oriented. Hierarchy begins with charts and metric categories. Grouping is by on-chain theme, market context, and dashboards. Progressive disclosure moves from chart to explanation to research. Density is high but cleaner than many crypto dashboards. Search/filtering help metric discovery. Workspace is analyst-dashboard oriented.

## 4. Visualization

Charts are the central product asset. Cards summarize dashboards and metrics. Heatmaps are less central. Timelines appear through cycle analysis. Tables support metric detail. Network graphs are not core. Animations are minimal. Typography is research-oriented. Color language is professional and chart-focused.

## 5. Decision Support

Users reach conclusions through metric history, cohort behavior, market-cycle context, and research explanation. Evidence is metric-backed. Reasoning appears in research and must be distinguished from raw metrics. Historical comparison is strong. Confidence is based on metric quality and interpretation. Counter-evidence comes through alternative metrics and research nuance.

## 6. UX

Learning curve is medium-high. Onboarding depends on metric education. Interaction is chart, dashboard, metric, and report driven. Customization is moderate. Desktop is primary. Mobile is better for reading/monitoring. Accessibility is supported by clean visual design but challenged by metric complexity.

## 7. Strengths

- Strong metric research context.
- Professional chart quality.
- Historical comparison.
- Trustworthy analytical posture.
- Good evidence-to-research bridge.

## 8. Weaknesses

- Metric complexity can intimidate.
- Less real-time trading workflow.
- Research interpretation may be too slow for tactical decisions.
- Requires strong user education.
