## 3. Information Architecture

Navigation is token, wallet, dashboard, and alert oriented. Hierarchy begins with tracked entities, tokens, flows, and smart-money indicators. Grouping is on-chain category and wallet segment driven. Progressive disclosure moves from dashboards to wallet/token detail. Density is high. Search and filtering are central. Workspace supports watchlists and alerts.

## 4. Visualization

Charts, tables, wallet lists, and flow views dominate. Cards summarize token and wallet activity. Heatmaps may support segment views. Timelines appear through transactions and flows. Network graphs are useful but not always primary. Typography and spacing support expert dashboards. Color language highlights inflow/outflow and segments.

## 5. Decision Support

Users reach conclusions by watching wallet cohorts, flows, labels, and token movements. Evidence is on-chain and label-backed. Reasoning is mostly user-driven. Historical context exists through wallet/token history. Confidence should depend on label and source quality. Counter-evidence includes conflicting flows or unlabeled activity.

## 6. UX

Learning curve is medium-high. Onboarding requires wallet/label literacy. Interaction is dashboard, filter, alert, search, and drilldown driven. Customization is strong for monitored entities. Desktop is primary. Mobile is monitoring-oriented. Accessibility can be challenged by table density.

## 7. Strengths

- Wallet label intelligence.
- Smart-money workflow.
- Strong alert/watchlist model.
- Useful on-chain segmentation.
- Research value for crypto-native users.

## 8. Weaknesses

- Label confidence can be overread.
- Less beginner-friendly.
- Dense dashboards require context.
- On-chain focus needs market/context pairing.
